## OMISSIONS
- Cliopatria (Seshat Global History Databank) is a commercially usable historical-borders dataset: about 14K records and 1,600+ polities from 3400 BCE to 2024 CE, each row with a Wikidata ID, licensed CC BY 4.0 (verified LICENSE.md: commercial use allowed, attribution required, no share-alike). It is the obvious base layer instead of GPL-3.0 historical-basemaps and would save months of polygon work. Source: https://github.com/Seshat-Global-History-Databank/cliopatria
- Natural Earth ships point-of-view (worldview) admin-0 files (ISO and TLC POVs in the CHANGELOG), all public domain. These answer the disputed-borders / 'per-viewer-country' question directly and the report did not mention them.
- OHM's hosted map styles include GlobCover land cover licensed for 'educational and/or scientific purposes' only. Never render OHM's hosted tiles or styles directly. Use the raw vector data, filter out CC BY-SA and any legacy NC-tagged objects, and expect patchy coverage for many eras and regions.
- MapLibre GL JS is now v6.11 (ESM-only, WebGL2-only, map.transform removed). Headless renders need WebGL2, via a GPU or SwiftShader. MapLibre Native (BSD-2) is a browserless server-side option worth benchmarking for render cost.
- Remotion fine print the report missed: using the Remotion Player puts you on Automators; users may not upload their own Remotion code; stills and thumbnails count as renders; a Remotion 5.0 draft change counts contractors and makes telemetry mandatory. Revideo and Motion Canvas (both MIT, verified) have no per-render license if you want to avoid vendor lock-in. Either way the license cost per video is pennies; the real cost is compute.
- The cost_items leave out the main per-video variable costs: TTS, render compute (GPU/CPU minutes for headless WebGL frame capture), forced alignment, music licensing, storage and egress. Open-weight options can bring TTS and alignment close to zero marginal cost: Kokoro-82M (Apache-licensed weights, verified), Chatterbox (MIT, with built-in Perth watermark, verified) and WhisperX (BSD-2, verified) for word timestamps.
- Near-zero-cost basemap hosting the report missed: Protomaps PMTiles (BSD-3) as a single planet file on object storage; OpenFreeMap (MIT, OSM data); Mapterhorn (BSD-3 code, open terrain tiles with per-source attribution), a newer alternative to Terrarium.
- Satellite imagery: EOX also has 2017, 2024 and 2025 mosaics, and the only CC BY one (2016) is ten years out of date. A cheaper-than-licensing route is building your own cloud-free mosaics from raw Copernicus Sentinel-2 or Landsat data, which are free for commercial use. That costs compute, not license fees.
- Music risk: YouTube's October 2024 rule blocks Shorts longer than 1 minute that have an active Content ID claim, and 61–90 s cuts get cross-posted to Shorts. Music must be Content-ID-safe and SaaS-licensed (most stock libraries forbid passing music through to customers). No music sourcing plan or cost appears in the report. Verify the current rule.
- EU AI Act Article 50 transparency duties (machine-readable marking of AI-generated audio and video) are scheduled to apply from 2 August 2026, subject to possible 'digital omnibus' transition changes. That likely makes C2PA or watermark marking of outputs mandatory for EU users, not optional 'where relevant'. Have counsel check the current status.
- The product needs an attribution engine: CC BY (Cliopatria, Daylight), ODbL produced-work notices (OSM), per-source Terrarium/Mapterhorn credits and NASA credits must be generated into each video's end card or description automatically.
- Voice cloning of users' own voices needs consent capture and likeness-law compliance (e.g., US state right-of-publicity / ELVIS-type laws), plus YouTube and TikTok disclosure when someone else's voice is cloned.
- Every legally decisive ToS/policy claim (Google Tiles, Earth Studio, Mapbox video, TikTok, Meta, YouTube, EOX, Pixabay, Cesium) is still unverified against primary pages, both by the researcher and in this check (egress was blocked). Budget a counsel review before launch.

## RELIABILITY
The report holds up where it could be checked, and it says openly which claims rest on search snippets. Every license and price I could load from a primary source was correct: GPL-3.0 for historical-basemaps, the Depth-Anything-V2 split, milsymbol, flubber, flag-icons and BiRefNet (MIT), Natural Earth (public domain), Protomaps and Terrarium attribution, OHM CC0 with BY/BY-SA exceptions, the Google Font licenses, Mapbox GL v3.9 rain/snow, MapLibre v5 globe/terrain, Remotion's $0.01/render with a $100 minimum, and the Claude API prices. I found no outright false statement. The weak spots are staleness and omissions. MapLibre is now v6, the EOX version range is out of date, OHM's hosted styles carry non-commercial GlobCover data, and the report misses Cliopatria (CC BY), Natural Earth's point-of-view files, Remotion's fine print, and the main per-video cost drivers (TTS, render compute, music). The Google, Mapbox, TikTok, Meta and YouTube policy claims carry the most legal weight, and none of them could be checked against primary pages in this environment (egress blocked). They match what I know, but treat them as provisional until counsel or someone with full web access confirms them.
