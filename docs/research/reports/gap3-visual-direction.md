**Bottom line**
1. **The reference corpus has not been measured yet, so I have not given you a fake one.** The research container's network proxy blocked YouTube, TikTok, arXiv, Wikipedia, Hugging Face, Prolific, remotion.dev, OpenReview, Crossref and the creator-blog sites. The session's web-search budget was also used up. The only sources I could reach were GitHub (raw files, code search), npm and PyPI. So I delivered four things instead:
   - (a) one measured short-form shot dataset of 4,853 shots;
   - (b) a tested measurement pipeline and annotation schema, ready to run on a machine that can reach YouTube;
   - (c) a reference-corpus manifest and per-channel style priors, every cell tagged with where it came from;
   - (d) a shot-grammar spec built on the best evidence available, with each parameter marked "replace after measurement" where needed.
2. **The strongest evidence supports one approach.** Templates written by human designers, with the LLM choosing only from a fixed list, timing driven by the audio, and a constraint solver. Three published systems used this kind of bounded selection with code-based rendering and got close to designer quality: FilmAgent, Data Player and Code2Video. Free-form LLM animation does not.
3. **Off-the-shelf AI image/video judges are not reliable enough for aesthetics.** On visualization quality, frontier models agree with expert ratings at only about r = 0.43–0.48. They are much better at choosing between two options than at giving absolute scores, and they are poor at recognizing camera motion. Use them to catch defects and to make pairwise choices, and calibrate them against human ratings. Do not use them as the quality bar.
4. **MVP template set.** 18 shared templates are estimated to cover about 86–98% of beats in each of the four launch presets. The work is about 17 motion-designer person-weeks plus about 18 engineering person-weeks (estimate).
5. **The direction layer is cheap to run.** About $0.18 per 60-second short and about $1.35 per 10-minute video on Claude Sonnet 5.5, or roughly half that through the Batch API. Arithmetic is in §8.

## 2. Reference corpus: status, measured baseline and measurement plan

### 2.1 What is actually measured (VERIFIED)

The AutoShot "SHOT" dataset (CVPR-W 2023, https://github.com/wentaozhu/AutoShot) contains 853 Kuaishou short videos with shot boundaries labelled by hand. The paper's Table 1 gives an average video length of 39.5 s and an average shot length of 2.59 s, against 6.57 s for BBC, 5.65 s for RAI and 15.34 s for ClipShots.

I recomputed the distribution from the public label file (`kuaishou_v2.txt`; 344 videos and 4,853 shots are parseable). I assumed 27 fps, which reproduces the paper's 2.59 s mean. The `-1` suffix in the table means one header line excluded.

| Statistic | Value |
|---|---|
| Median shot | **1.67 s** |
| Mean shot | 2.55 s |
| p10 / p25 / p75 / p90 | 0.44 / 0.85 / 3.00 / 4.78 s |
| Lognormal fit | μ = 0.448, **σ = 0.99** (median 1.57 s) |
| Shots under 1 s | 30% |
| Shots over 5 s | 9% |
| Cut rate | 21–23 cuts per minute (median / mean) |

**Caveat:** these are general short videos (including ads and game clips), not map content. I use them as an upper bound on short-form cut rate.

### 2.3 Measurement plan (run on your laptop or another machine that can reach YouTube)

**Step 1: automatic pass with `measure_corpus.py`.**
- Hard cuts: PySceneDetect content and adaptive detectors, with each cut confirmed by checking whether one global motion still explains both frames (this rejects fast pans).
- Camera segments: an affine flow model measured about the frame centre, so a zoom is not mistaken for a pan. Output is static / pan / zoom in / zoom out / rotate / compound.
- Visual events: localized new-pixel blobs left over after compensating for camera motion.
- Optional word timestamps (faster-whisper), giving signed **visual-to-word offsets** where negative means the visual leads.

**Step 2: VLM pass.** Claude Sonnet 5.5 tags primitives, beat type and how many elements are entering, using 3 keyframes plus the transcript window.
- Cost: about 3 × 442 image tokens + 2.1k text in, 300 out. That is about $0.0099 per shot, roughly $16 for about 1,600 shots, or about $8 through the Batch API.

**Step 3: audio pass and human check.** Librosa onset and novelty analysis finds SFX hits and music events. A motion designer then verifies 10–20% of rows. This matters because VLMs misread camera motion (§5.1).

**Corpus size.** A first-order camera-transition matrix of 7×7 needs about 100 transitions per row to estimate each row within ±0.1. That is about 700–1,000 transitions, or about 85 minutes of long-form per preset at about 12 segments per minute. **So expand from 15 videos to about 40–60 (5–8 per preset)** before fitting per-preset matrices.

**Legal flag.** YouTube's Terms of Service restrict downloading. Decide between counsel-approved internal analysis, annotation while streaming, or licensing footage from a creator partner. This is a founder question.

## 3. Shot grammar spec (replaces Area 1's estimates; full table in `shot_grammar_spec.csv`)

### 3.1 Shot length and density by format and preset

Durations are drawn from a lognormal with the stated median and σ, then constrained by the beat length (§6).

| Format / preset | Median shot or segment | σ | p10–p90 | New elements per min | Visual-mode change | Macro interrupt | Music events per min | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Short 9:16 (30–90 s)** | 1.8 s | 0.9 | 0.6–4.5 s | 30–50 | every 3–6 s | first visual change ≤1 s | 2–4 | M (SHOT), nudged longer for map flights (R) |
| P1 Documentary satellite (RealLifeLore / Johnny Harris type) | 4.5 s | 0.7 | 1.5–11 s | 15–25 | 8–15 s | 30–60 s | 1.5–3 | V + R |
| P2 Campaign / battle (Kings and Generals / Epic History type) | 6.0 s | 0.7 | 2–16 s | 10–20 | 10–20 s | 45–90 s | 1–2 | R |
| P3 Flat explainer (PolyMatter / Wendover type) | 6.0 s | 0.8 | 2–20 s, plus 40–60 s data holds | 12–20 | 6–12 s | 30–60 s | 1–2 | SECONDARY + R |
| P4 Timelapse (EmperorTigerstar / Ollie Bye type) | continuous map; reframe every 10–60 s | – | – | 1–4 border events per s at peak | chapter card per era | 60–180 s | 0.5–1 | R |

Rules that apply to every format:
- No more than 2 new elements may enter in any 0.5 s window (3 for a group of unit blocks).
- Keep the current idea plus at most one earlier element on screen (Vox rule, SECONDARY).
- Never use the same template more than twice in a row, or the same camera move three times in a row (P4 excepted).

### 3.2 Synchronisation rules (evidence-graded)

| Rule | Value | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| The element that depicts a spoken referent (label, highlight, pin) appears **0–200 ms before** the word starts; it should never lag by more than about 100 ms | lead −200…0 ms | Data Player's formative study with 10 professional designers: animations are triggered at, and last for, the matching narration segment (VERIFIED, arXiv 2308.04703). Listeners look at a referent about 200 ms after the word starts (eye-tracking "visual world" research, Allopenna et al. 1998; R, not re-verified). Simultaneous narration and animation beats successive presentation (Mayer's temporal contiguity, R). |
| Camera **settles at least 300 ms before** the key noun, so labels land on a stable frame | ≥300 ms | R, consistent with Vox "elements fast, cameras slow" (SECONDARY) |
| SFX hit sits on the visual impact frame, within ±1 frame (33 ms at 30 fps). Sound must not lead picture by more than 45 ms or lag by more than 125 ms | −45…+125 ms limits | ITU-R BT.1359 detectability (+45 / −125 ms) and acceptability (+90 / −185 ms) (SECONDARY: github.com/walterfan/webrtc_note) |
| Whoosh starts 2–4 frames before the camera's peak velocity; the cut lands at peak velocity | – | Vox process (SECONDARY) |
| Labels sit **on** the map region, not in a side panel | – | Spatial-contiguity meta-analysis: 58 comparisons, n = 2,426, g = 0.63 (Schroeder & Cenkci 2018, doi:10.1007/s10648-018-9435-9; SECONDARY via learning-wiki) |
| Text stays on screen at least max(1.0 s, 0.3 s per word + 0.5 s) | – | R |
| Army arrows and unit moves start on the movement verb and run through the clause | lead −200…+300 ms | R (P2) |

### 3.3 Camera grammar

- **Flight durations are computed, not chosen by the LLM.** MapLibre's `flyTo` implements van Wijk & Nuij's (2003) optimal zoom-and-pan path. Its default curve of 1.42 is "the average value selected by participants in the user study", and its default speed is 1.2 screenfuls per second (VERIFIED: maplibre-gl 6.11.2 `src/ui/camera.ts`; paper https://www.win.tue.nl/~vanwijk/zoompan.pdf).
- Duration = path length S divided by speed V, with V set per preset, clamped to [1.5, 6] s and to the beat length. If the flight cannot fit the beat, the director inserts a cut or zoom-through instead.
- Pushes run 2–5% over the hold. 3D orbits run 5–15° over 4–8 s.
- A prior camera-transition matrix (for example, a flight is followed by a hold or push 60% of the time) is in `camera_transition_prior.csv`. It is labelled R and is to be replaced by measured Markov counts.

### 3.4 "Hero beats"

Templates alone produce competent videos; the wow factor comes from a few set pieces. Budget **one hero shot every 60–90 s in long-form and one per short**, placed at the narrative peak (data-video narrative structure, Amini et al. CHI 2015, doi:10.1145/2702123.2702431, bibliographic data VERIFIED, content R). Examples: a 3D terrain orbit with rain, snow or fog; a sweep of mass army arrows; a full border morph. These cost more to render and are rationed by the planner.

### 5.1 How reliable are VLM judges? (VERIFIED unless marked)

| Benchmark | Finding |
|---|---|
| **VisJudgeBench** (ICLR 2026; 3,090 expert-rated visualizations, including choropleth and point maps) | Correlation with experts: **GPT-5 0.428, GPT-4o 0.482, Claude-4-Sonnet 0.465, Gemini-2.5-Pro 0.265.** A fine-tuned 7B model reaches 0.687. (github.com/HKUSTDial/VisJudgeBench) |
| **MLLM-as-a-Judge** (ICML 2024) | GPT-4V **pairwise accuracy without ties 0.773**, with ties 0.636; scoring Pearson 0.490. The paper states MLLMs show "human-like discernment in Pair Comparison" but "significant divergence" in scoring and batch ranking. (arXiv 2402.04788) |
| **Video-Bench** (CVPR 2025; SECONDARY notes) | GPT-4o with few-shot prompting: aesthetic quality Spearman 0.702, **motion quality 0.514, temporal consistency 0.402**. Machine-to-human agreement Krippendorff α 0.50, against 0.52 between humans. |
| **VideoScore** (EMNLP 2024) | Spearman 77.1 in-domain on VideoFeedback-test, "beating the prior best metrics by about 50 points". It is trained for AI-generated realistic video, not motion graphics. |
| **VideoScore2** | 44.35 accuracy in-domain, 50.37 average across out-of-domain benchmarks. |

**Conclusion.** Use VLMs for pairwise choices and for defect detection: overlaps, unreadable text, wrong country highlighted, elements off-screen. Do not use them to set the aesthetic bar. Measure direction quality from the plan itself: shot-length distribution against the target lognormal (KS distance), sync offsets, element density, repetition. All of these can be computed exactly from our own timeline.

### 5.2 Human-preference protocol

**Primary design: audio-locked pairwise comparison.**
- Our engine directs visuals onto the reference excerpt's own narration and music track, using word timestamps. Raters compare our visuals with the original's under **identical audio**, so only direction differs.
- Fallback if counsel says no to using reference clips: commission pro-designer "gold" clips on our own script and voiceover (ESTIMATE $150–400 per finished minute; not verified).

**Stimuli.**
- 30–60 s excerpts for long-form; full shorts for 9:16.
- **At least 20 and ideally 40 distinct clip pairs per arm.** Variance between clips caps the effective sample size at roughly k/ρ: with 12 clips and ρ = 0.1, no more than about 120 effective judgments however many raters you add.

**Raters.**
- Prolific, with a custom screener (watches history or geography YouTube weekly; English-fluent; 18–45).
- Each rater does 10 pairs in about 25 min.
- Attention checks: a duplicated pair with order swapped, and an obvious-defect gold pair.
- Primary question: "Which would you rather keep watching?" (with a "same" option).
- Secondary questions: "more professional", "easier to follow".
- An optional comprehension quiz, in the style of TeachQuiz.

**Analysis.** Bradley-Terry model with rater and clip random effects. Report win rate (ties split half and half) with cluster-robust confidence intervals.

**Sample size (arithmetic).**
- Non-inferiority test, one-sided, α = 0.05, power 0.8. H0: p ≤ 0.35, H1: p = 0.45.
- n = [(1.645·√(0.35·0.65) + 0.842·√(0.45·0.55)) / 0.10]² = **145 effective judgments**.
- With 40 clips × 10 judgments, the design effect is 1 + (10−1)·0.1 = 1.9, so 400 judgments give 210 effective. ✔
- 95% CI half-width at n = 400: ±4.9 points.

**Cost per arm (arithmetic).**
- Prolific fee: 42.8% for corporate accounts, 33.3% for academic. Pay floor $8/h, recommended ≥$12/h. (SECONDARY transcription of prolific.com/pricing dated 2026-09-20, github.com/api-evangelist/prolific; also researcher-help article 445239.)
- One judgment ≈ 2.5 min → reward $0.50 at $12/h → **$0.714 with the fee**.
- 400 judgments = $286, plus about 10% for screening ≈ **$315 per arm**.
- A full launch round of 4 presets × 2 formats × 2 comparisons (against reference or gold, and against a slideshow baseline) = 16 arms × $315 ≈ **$5.0k**.
- A minimal round (2 presets × 2 formats × 1 comparison) ≈ **$1.3k**.

**Expert panel.** 3 motion designers rate 20 videos on 8 rubric dimensions: sync, camera grammar, layout and readability, variety, art-direction consistency, hero moments, geographic correctness, audio mix. Target agreement α ≥ 0.6. Cost ≈ 3 × 6 h × $60 ≈ **$1.1k per round** (ESTIMATE).

**VLM calibration.**
- Add every human pair (aim for 1,000 or more over the first rounds) to a calibration set.
- Run the VLM pairwise judge on it: about $0.031 per pair on Sonnet 5.5 (6 contact sheets × 1,844 tokens + 2k prompt in, 500 out), or $0.016 batched.
- Promote the VLM to an automated gate only where its agreement with the human majority is ≥ 0.70 and Cohen's κ ≥ 0.4 (±2.7 points at n = 1,000).
- Recalibrate whenever the model or a preset changes.

### 5.3 Launch quality bar (per preset × format)

1. **Against references (audio-locked) or pro gold:** win rate (ties split) ≥ 0.40 **and** a one-sided 95% lower bound > 0.35. In plain terms: not clearly worse than professional work by more than 15 points.
2. **Against an "AI slideshow" baseline** on the same script: win rate ≥ 0.75.
3. **Critical defects** ≤ 2%, audited on 150 or more renders. Critical means wrong geography, key label overlapping, or sync error over 250 ms. By the rule of three, 0 defects in 150 gives an upper bound of 2%.
4. **Channel-template test.** Raters see 3 videos from one synthetic channel on different topics and are asked "made from the same template?". The "yes" rate must not be significantly above that of a real human-made channel's videos. Consistent style is fine; interchangeable structure is not.
5. **After launch:** design partners' YouTube average view percentage stays within 10 points of their channel median.

## 6. Recommended direction architecture

1. **Beat segmentation.** An LLM splits the script into beats (a clause or sentence). Each beat gets: type (`BT.*`, 20 types), referents (places, people, numbers, dates), a verb for motion beats, and emphasis. TTS word timestamps fix every beat's start and end. Audio is the clock.
2. **Retrieval.** Pull the k nearest annotated beat sequences from the corpus (same preset, same beat-type n-grams) as few-shot examples (cached prompt).
3. **Director (Sonnet 5.5, structured output).** For each beat, it picks a `template_id` from an **enum limited to the preset and beat type**, fills in parameters (targets, framing, variant options) and marks hero beats. It never writes animation code at runtime.
4. **Variety engine.**
   - Sample variants and seeds under the corpus-fitted camera Markov prior.
   - Hard rules: no more than 2 identical templates in a row, no more than 3 identical camera moves in a row.
   - Hit the pattern-interrupt cadence from §3.1.
   - Keep a **per-customer channel history** and penalize template-sequence 3-grams and keyframe embeddings that are too close to that customer's recent videos.

   This directly answers YouTube's monetization policy, which (VERIFIED, support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392; renamed "inauthentic content" on 15 July 2025) says generic or repetitive content "includes content that looks like it's made with a template". It also lists "AI-generated content made with generic or unoriginal templates giving the impression of mass production" as ineligible.
5. **Timing and layout solver.**
   - Integer or constraint programming over frame-level events, applying the §3.2 offsets, van Wijk flight durations, minimum holds and the 0.5 s entry cap.
   - Layout uses a **6×6 anchor grid**, map-label collision avoidance and per-aspect safe zones (9:16 UI overlays). Re-anchoring handles 16:9, 9:16, 1:1 and 4:5.
6. **Best-of-N at the plan level (N = 3, text only).**
   - Score each plan with programmatic grammar metrics: KS distance to the target lognormal, sync violations, density, repetition.
   - Then a pairwise LLM judge picks the winner.
   - Render once.
7. **Render QA.**
   - Render keyframe contact sheets first (cheap stills).
   - A VLM checks for defects.
   - MoVer-style declarative checks run on the timeline.
   - Only failing beats are re-planned.
8. **Feedback.** Human rounds and design-partner retention data recalibrate the priors, the VLM gate and the template weights.

## KEY RECOMMENDATIONS
- Build direction from human-designed templates and let the LLM choose only from a fixed list (template_id enum plus parameters, via a JSON schema); FilmAgent, Data Player and Code2Video show bounded selection reaches near-designer quality while free-form generation does not.
- Drive all timing from TTS word timestamps through a constraint solver: visuals lead the word by 0-200 ms, the camera settles at least 300 ms before the key noun, and SFX land within ±1 frame of the visual hit; LLMs are unreliable at timing, and these limits come from published sync and design evidence.
- Compute map flight durations with the van Wijk & Nuij formula (MapLibre flyTo, curve 1.42, speed tuned per preset) clamped to the beat length, so camera motion is physically smooth and never chosen by the LLM.
- Use a 6x6 layout anchor grid with per-aspect safe zones, because Code2Video's ablation shows anchors raise layout scores from 45 to 83 while letting the model place elements itself scores 49.
- Ship a variety engine with corpus-fitted camera-transition priors and per-customer channel memory, because YouTube's policy explicitly demonetizes content that 'looks like it's made with a template'.
- Run best-of-N (N=3) on the cheap text plan, scored by programmatic grammar metrics plus a pairwise LLM judge, and render only the winner; rendering N videos costs far more.
- Use VLMs only to detect defects and make pairwise choices until they are calibrated against at least 1,000 human pairs; frontier VLMs agree with experts at only r of about 0.43-0.48 on visualization aesthetics and score 36 AP on camera motion.
- Measure the corpus before freezing the grammar: expand to 40-60 videos (5-8 per preset) and run measure_corpus.py plus VLM tagging (about $16) plus 10-20% verification by a motion designer, because the current long-form parameters are mostly priors.
- Adopt the 18-template MVP (estimated 86-98% beat coverage per preset) and staff about 17 motion-designer and 18 engineering person-weeks, because templates are the main driver of perceived quality.
- Set the launch bar as audio-locked pairwise tests with at least 0.40 win rate against reference or pro excerpts (lower bound above 0.35), at least 0.75 against a slideshow baseline, at most 2% critical defects, and a channel-template test; this makes 'astonishing' measurable.
- Budget one hero shot every 60-90 s (3D terrain with weather, mass arrows, border morph) placed at the narrative peak, because templates deliver competence while set pieces deliver the wow factor.
- Use Prolific with a custom screener, 40 clip pairs × 10 judgments per arm (about $315), keeping at least 20 distinct clips because variance between clips, not rater count, limits statistical power.

## RISKS
- The reference corpus is unmeasured: the long-form shot-length, transition and sync parameters for Kings and Generals, Epic History, RealLifeLore and the timelapse channels are recall estimates. Freezing them could encode a wrong rhythm. Mitigation: measure 40-60 videos before shipping the grammar.
- YouTube Terms of Service restrict downloading, so running yt-dlp to measure reference videos, or using reference clips in Prolific studies, carries legal and ToS risk. This needs a counsel decision; alternatives are annotation while streaming, commissioned gold clips or licensed footage from a creator partner.
- A templated look can trigger demonetization: YouTube explicitly penalizes content that 'looks like it's made with a template'. A small template library reused across a customer's channel is exactly that pattern unless the variety engine and channel memory work and are measured.
- VLM judges create false confidence: correlation with experts on visualization aesthetics is only about 0.43-0.48, about 0.51 for motion quality and weak for camera motion. Automated quality gates may pass bland or broken videos unless they are calibrated against humans.
- Bounded selection can make videos bland: constraining the LLM to templates reliably delivers competence, but 'astonishing' depends on the quality of the hero templates and the taste of one or two designers.
- The evidence comes from neighbouring fields (educational Manim, data videos, 3D film-making); none of it evaluates map storytelling directly, so the effect sizes may not transfer.
- The rater pool may not match the real audience: Prolific raters are not the history and geography audience, and preferences, especially for pacing, may differ. Use a screener, then validate with design partners' retention data.
- Statistical power is limited by the number of distinct clips, not raters (needs at least 20-40 clip pairs per arm); commissioning gold clips for every preset can get expensive.
- Sync accuracy depends on TTS and ASR word-timestamp quality; errors of 50-100 ms eat most of the sync budget.
- The effort estimate may be low for the heavy templates (unit blocks, terrain plus weather effects, timelapse morphs); geometry such as equal-area size comparison and border morphing between historical datasets carries engineering risk.
- Several prior-art details (MoVer's gains, GeoCamera, Amini et al., eye-tracking latency, Remotion license terms, Hera's status) could not be re-verified because the network blocked them.
