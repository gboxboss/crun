## OMISSIONS
- Cheaper narration: Google's own TTS pricing page lists Gemini TTS at about $0.009–0.018 per minute (Flash-Lite preview $6/M audio tokens until 31 Dec 2026, then $12/M). That is a third to two-thirds of the Chirp 3 HD cost. Chirp 3 HD also includes 1M free characters a month. Open-weight Apache-2.0 TTS such as Kokoro-82M can run on our own CPU/GPU at almost no marginal cost. Benchmark voice quality before you lock in COGS.
- Render engine choice: HyperFrames (HeyGen, Apache-2.0, about 55k stars) and MapLibre GL JS (BSD) plus headless Chromium and FFmpeg avoid Remotion's licence entirely. Remotion's terms count contractors toward the 3-person limit, bill thumbnails and server-side previews as renders, forbid letting users upload their own Remotion code, and a 5.0 licence change is pending. If GSAP is used, check its post-Webflow 'standard no-charge' licence for any clause excluding no-code visual builders, since our scene editor could fall under it.
- Free watermarking for EU Art. 50: Meta's Video Seal (MIT) and AudioSeal can supply the 'imperceptible watermark' layer at no licence cost. Google models (Veo, Imagen, Lyria, Gemini) already embed SynthID, but our programmatic map frames and the final composite will not carry it. Art. 50(2) clearly covers our AI-generated narration (synthetic audio) and scripts even if the map visuals turn out to be out of scope, so compliance is needed whatever the 'is a stylized map synthetic?' legal opinion says.
- C2PA signing: YouTube appears to show 'Made with AI' only for manifests from trust-listed signers; self-signed manifests were ignored in the Sept-2026 test. Public YouTube streams strip C2PA. Choosing a signing certificate is therefore both a compliance decision and a product-perception decision. Budget for a trust-listed certificate early.
- Map and data licensing alternatives the report missed: OpenHistoricalMap data is CC0, which is far safer than the GPL-3.0 aourednik borders. Natural Earth (public domain) ships 'point-of-view' boundary sets for about 30 countries, ready-made for the disputed-border presets. CShapes 2.0 (the common 1886–2019 state borders set) is reportedly CC BY-NC-SA, so non-commercial; check before use. For a satellite/terrain look: NASA Blue Marble is public domain; EOX Sentinel-2 cloudless is CC BY only for the 2016 edition, and later editions are NC-SA and need a commercial licence; Esri and Google imagery need explicit permission for video export. Self-hosted Protomaps/OpenFreeMap OSM tiles remove MapTiler/Mapbox session fees and redistribution ambiguity (keep ODbL attribution).
- Music cost and Content ID: Vertex Lyria 3 costs $0.04 per 30-s clip and Lyria 3 Pro $0.08 per full song (not on Google's indemnity list). That is a cheap way to make unique per-video music, though its Content ID behaviour is untested. Never pipe YouTube Audio Library tracks through the SaaS.
- Auto-posting constraints, with numbers: uploads through the YouTube Data API from unverified projects are locked to PRIVATE until Google completes a compliance audit. The default quota is 10,000 units a day and video uploads are expensive in quota (historically 1,600 units). Unaudited TikTok Content Posting API clients can only post privately (SELF_ONLY). Both audits must be planned before 'auto-post' is marketed.
- Platform format rules affect the cutdown feature. YouTube treats vertical or square uploads of up to 3 minutes as Shorts, and Shorts are paid from the Shorts revenue pool, not long-form RPM. Shorts over 1 minute with a Content ID claim get blocked. TikTok Creator Rewards needs 10k followers and is open only in certain countries, which limits value for the non-English segments the report wants to target. TikTok's new 'see less AI-generated content' feed control is a reach risk.
- Copyright in outputs: under US Copyright Office guidance (2025), mostly AI-generated video/script content may not be protectable without enough human authorship. Users may be unable to enforce rights or use Content ID on their own videos. This strengthens the case for the human-edit step and the authorship log, and the terms of service must allocate rights and indemnities clearly.
- Competitor gaps: the report does not check older animated-map SaaS (for example Animaps and Mapbox-based map-video makers) or Google's own Aerial View and Photorealistic 3D Tiles terms, whose caching and export restrictions likely forbid rendering them into downloadable videos. Most competitor prices remain secondary. Re-verify every competitor price on the live pages before publishing comparison pages.
- Cheaper LLM routing: Gemini 3.8 Flash costs $0.75/$3.75 per M tokens (introductory, until 31 Dec 2026; $1.50/$7.50 after) and Claude Haiku 4.5 costs $1/$5. Either could handle scene planning and QA, leaving Sonnet 5.5 for the script only. Combined with batch and caching, this could halve LLM COGS, which is the largest fixed per-video cost for Shorts.
- Unit-economics caveat: the Pro tier includes premium voices, AI imagery and hero shots. The report's own premium COGS ($0.60–1.30/min) exceeds Pro's $0.79 per included minute unless the credit surcharges are enforced strictly. Free unlimited previews must be client-side (browser Player), not server renders, or they become an uncapped cost (and, on Remotion, billable renders).

## RELIABILITY
The report is mostly reliable on the points that decide cost and legality. Every Google Cloud (Veo, Imagen, TTS), Anthropic and Remotion price I could check against a primary source or the vendor's own package was accurate. The quoted YouTube policy wording and TikTok's 1-minute rule hold. The EU AI Act dates and fine levels are correct. The errors are mostly over-statements. The EU Code of Practice is presented as mandatory and as conferring a 'presumption of conformity', and it is neither. YouTube's C2PA labelling is described as automatic for any 'made with AI' manifest, when only trusted signers seem to trigger it. Chirp 3 HD's free first million characters is described as a price tier. California AB 2655, which was struck down, is cited as live law. Zebracat's 5-minute cap is applied to every tier. Competitor pricing, channel statistics and market-traction figures (Higgsfield, Crayo, the 16 terminated channels) rest almost entirely on AI-assisted secondary notes. They are unverifiable here, and Vyond is labelled [V] when it is not. The biggest gaps are missed cheaper or safer options: Gemini or open-weight TTS, Apache-licensed render engines, free watermarking, CC0/public-domain map data, Lyria music, and the YouTube and TikTok API audit limits. The strategic conclusions still hold. Treat the competitor numbers as leads to check, not facts.
