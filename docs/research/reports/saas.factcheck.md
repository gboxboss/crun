## OMISSIONS
- The fixed-cost table understates the Remotion license cost of per-scene rendering. Each separate scene render, thumbnail and re-render after an edit is a chargeable $0.01 render, so about 21 renders per video at 10k videos/month is about $2.1k/month, not $100–300. Options: render the whole video in one renderMediaOnLambda call (1 render) and use the scene cache only for previews and assets, or negotiate Enterprise per-render pricing.
- Remotion 5.0 Terms take effect when v5 ships. Automators licensees must set a license key with per-render telemetry, or submit verifiable monthly reports. Client-side renders always send telemetry, including the end user's IP, which must go in the privacy policy as a recipient. Budget for the terms changing again, because Remotion is source-available, not OSI open source.
- Remotion's headcount threshold counts freelancers, contractors and part-timers who operate the codebase. The report's recommended beta team (founder, two engineers, a motion designer and a part-time LLM engineer) is 4 or more people, so a Company License ($100/month minimum plus per-render fees) applies from beta, not from 'scale'.
- The Remotion license does not cover codec patents (H.264/HEVC/AAC), and the FAQ puts that responsibility on the licensee. A SaaS encoding H.264 at volume should get counsel's view on AVC/AAC pool licensing.
- Remotion forbids letting end users bring or upload their own Remotion code. AI-generated code that users then edit is allowed. This rules out a 'custom code' power-user mode or a user-submitted template marketplace unless Remotion approves it in writing.
- AWS Lambda has no GPU, according to Remotion's own docs, so 3D globe and terrain scenes on Lambda render in software. New AWS accounts can have concurrency far below 1,000, and GPU EC2 quotas also need requests. File the quota requests in week 1, not at launch.
- Cheaper render options were not costed. Cloud Run Delayed Jobs cost $0.0000126/vCPU-s (about 30% below standard; prices can change every 30 days), worker pools $0.000011244/vCPU-s, and committed-use discounts go down to $0.00000972. Spot or preemptible VMs and dedicated bare-metal CPU servers (e.g., Hetzner) are often 2–5x cheaper than serverless for steady bulk rendering. Cloud Run Jobs bill a 1-minute minimum per instance, so sub-minute scene chunks run as separate job tasks are billed as a full minute.
- EU AI Act Article 50(4) deployer duties fall on your customers, and the product should support them. Realistic AI imagery of real people, places or events counts as a deepfake and needs a visible disclosure. AI-generated text published to inform the public on matters of public interest (history and geopolitics explainers) must be disclosed unless a human reviewed it and holds editorial responsibility. Ship visible-label tooling and record human review. Fines reach €15M or 3% of turnover.
- The final EU Code of Practice (10 Jun 2026) treats watermarking as a required layer alongside C2PA metadata for signatories. The report's 'watermark later' weakens the presumption of conformity. C2PA also needs a production signing certificate from a trust-listed issuer; self-signed certificates show 'signer not in trust list'. Neither the cost nor the lead time is budgeted.
- Llama Guard 4 is pruned from Llama 4 Scout, so the Llama 4 terms appear to apply, and the Llama 4 Use Policy withholds multimodal-model rights from EU-based companies. If the company is EU-incorporated, use gpt-oss-safeguard (Apache-2.0) or a hosted classifier instead.
- Merchant-of-record economics: Polar fees are charged on the VAT-inclusive total, plus payout fees ($2/month active, 0.25% + $0.25 per payout, 0.25–1% FX). Polar can refund unilaterally within 60 days and suspend accounts near a 0.7% chargeback ratio. Effective take on EU sales is about 10–11% at $20 price points. Get the MoR's written approval for 'AI video about wars, extremism history and politics' before building billing.
- OpenAI's free moderation does not classify audio. Generated TTS and any uploaded voice or music need separate checks, at least by moderating the script text that produced them. Its own docs example flags a war-movie frame as violence 0.86, which confirms that raw flags cannot be used as block decisions for this genre.
- Batch and caching discounts are missing from the per-video cost plan. The Anthropic Batch API is 50% off, Gemini has batch and flex modes, and prompt caching cuts repeated system prompts. Non-urgent steps (policy checks, VLM QA, the research summarization in 'overnight' mode) can roughly halve LLM cost.
- Single-vendor price risk: Trigger.dev's compute and plan prices, which drive the '3.9x Cloud Run' claim, could not be verified from a primary source. Run a 1-week billing test before committing.
