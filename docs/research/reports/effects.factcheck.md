## OMISSIONS
- Architecture problem: deck.gl layers interleaved in MapLibre are NOT draped over MapLibre terrain, and camera roll and non-default FOV are not synced (deck.gl maplibre overview). Globe interleaving uses the experimental GlobeView, where TextLayer and non-billboard IconLayer are hidden by default. Military arrows, trips and unit icons on 3D-terrain or globe shots therefore need MapLibre-native line/fill/custom layers or billboard icons. Remove roll from the camera IR, or only use it in non-deck.gl scenes.
- Remotion licence cost per video is higher than $0.01. The QA still per scene, per-scene regenerations and possibly each self-chunked renderMedia call all count as renders. Using the Player in the app puts you in 'Automators' once you are over 3 people, and Remotion 5.0 makes licence-key telemetry mandatory. Ask Remotion in writing how distributed chunk renders are counted before designing the GPU pool.
- Remotion Cloud Run is alpha and not actively developed, and Lambda has no GPU. If you want serverless GPU, plan your own container (for example on a scale-to-zero GPU platform) rather than relying on Remotion's Cloud Run package.
- Cheaper compute options were not considered: spot or preemptible GPU instances, several Chrome processes per GPU instance (concurrency=1 leaves a T4 underused), lower-cost GPU hosts outside AWS, and Remotion client-side rendering (renderMediaOnWeb). Client-side rendering moves the cost to the user's GPU. It still counts as a billable render, supports ThreeCanvas but not mix-blend-mode, and I did not verify it with a raw MapLibre canvas. 2D d3/SVG presets could render cheaply on Lambda (CPU) with no WebGL at all.
- Historical borders: OpenHistoricalMap data is CC0 (community-mapped, uneven coverage). Wikidata (CC0) can serve as the gazetteer for places and polities with dates. geoBoundaries (CC BY 4.0) covers modern admin boundaries. Commercial historical atlases such as Euratlas or GeaCron can be licensed. The 'no clean dataset' gap is smaller than stated, but curation is still needed.
- Satellite style is possible without a paid licence: EOX Sentinel-2 cloudless 2016 (CC BY 4.0) or your own mosaic from Copernicus Sentinel-2 (free, commercial use allowed). NASA Blue Marble is only about 500 m resolution, so it cannot support close zooms.
- Disputed borders and geopolitical sensitivity: Natural Earth publishes 'point-of-view' boundary variants. A geography/war SaaS needs a policy for Kashmir, Crimea, Taiwan, Western Sahara and similar regions, and probably per-market worldviews.
- Per-video cost scope: the report covers only the render stage. TTS, LLM scripting and storyboard calls, vision-QA calls, AI image generation, music and SFX licensing, stock or archival media, storage and egress are all missing, and TTS, image generation or media licensing will likely dominate unit cost.
- Platform-policy risk for customers: YouTube's Partner Program policy on mass-produced or 'inauthentic' content (tightened in 2025) and its AI/synthetic-content disclosure rules could demonetize auto-generated videos. I did not verify this in this session. Check the current policy and build in variation and human-editing features.
- Media and IP licensing beyond code: Wikimedia Commons files each have their own licence, and photos of artworks raise copyright questions that vary by country. AI-generated likenesses of real people carry publicity and defamation risk. Fonts must be OFL or commercially licensed. Iconify has 51 CC BY 4.0 sets, CC BY-SA and GPL sets, and one CC BY-NC-SA set, not just Game Icons, so enforce a licence allowlist.
- AI model training-data provenance: Apache or MIT code licences (LaMa, BiRefNet, Depth Anything Small) do not cover the training datasets. Some, such as Places for LaMa, have research-oriented terms. Have counsel review, or use a commercial API, if indemnification matters.
- Dependency roadmap risk: deck.gl 9.4 is expected to be the last v9 release, and v10 brings breaking changes (luma.gl v10, loaders.gl v5). Chromium may remove SwiftShader on Linux beyond the opt-in flag. Pin Chrome-for-Testing, MapLibre and deck.gl versions per render-worker image, and add golden-frame tests.
- Possible determinism trap to test: with time frozen via setNow and any non-zero style or paint transitions left on, MapLibre may never reach 'idle', which causes 30 s delayRender timeouts. Enforce zero transitions in the preset compiler.

## RELIABILITY
The report is mostly reliable on library-level facts. Every npm version and date, every licence field, the MapLibre source-code claims (setNow, fadeDuration forcing full placement, prevPlacement variable anchors, WebGL2-only, maxCanvasSize), the Remotion licence terms and pricing, the Depth Anything, BiRefNet and historical-basemaps licences, the Mapbox licence and the GSAP clause all checked out against primary or near-primary sources. Pricing for third-party services (Cesium, MapTiler, AWS) is correct as far as mirrors and AWS's own Karpenter table show. Mapbox pricing and the Mapbox Product Terms could not be verified because those sites were blocked. There are four decision-relevant errors. (1) The proposed layer stack assumes deck.gl overlays sit correctly on MapLibre terrain and globe, which deck.gl's docs say they do not (no draping on MapLibre terrain, no roll sync, icons and text culled on globe). (2) The Remotion licence cost of about $0.01 per video understates it, because QA stills and possibly chunk renders are billable. (3) Remotion Cloud Run is called mature when it is an unmaintained alpha. (4) Google 3D Tiles are described as 'unclear' when the policy effectively prohibits this use. Several open, commercially safe data options were also missed (OpenHistoricalMap CC0, EOX 2016, Copernicus). The GPU cost-per-video figure is an unbenchmarked and probably optimistic estimate. Treat it as order-of-magnitude only.
